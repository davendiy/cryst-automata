For the sake of succinctness, we consider only two families of matrices from $\mathcal{Y}$ for $G_{24}$.

The first family falls into the case $\tr(Y) = \tau(Y) = 0$; its determinant is the cubic polynomial
No.~7 in Table~\ref{tab:case2_srdegrees0}:
\begin{align*}
f_\sigma(y_0, y_1, y_2) &= 4 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{2}^{2} + 4 \, y_{0}^{2} + 2 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{1} \\
& + 4 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0}^{2} + {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{1} + y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{2} - 1.
\end{align*}
It factorizes as
$$
f_\sigma(y_0, y_1, y_2) = (2y_0 - 1)(2y_2 + 1)(2y_0 + 2y_1 + 2y_2 + 1),
$$
so all its values are odd. Conversely, every odd integer $n$ is attained: taking
$y_0 = \frac{n+1}{2}$, $y_1 = -\frac{n+1}{2}$, $y_2 = 0$ gives $f_\sigma = n \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = n$.
Since $\tr(Y) = \tau(Y) = 0$, every value with $|f_\sigma| > 1$ is a self-replicating degree, and hence
$$
SCD_1 = \{1, 3, 5, \dots\}, \qquad SRD_1 = \{3, 5, 7, \dots\}.
$$

The second family is block-triangular, with the triple $(p_\sigma, q_\sigma, g_\sigma)$ from
Table~\ref{tab:case1_srdegrees0}:
$$
p_\sigma(y_0, y_1, y_2) = -2 y_1 - 1
$$
$$
q_\sigma(y_0, y_1, y_2) = 4 y_0^2 - 2(2 y_0 + 1) y_1 - 2 (2 y_0 + 1) y_2 - 1, \qquad
g_\sigma = 0.
$$
The polynomial $q_\sigma$ factorizes as
$$
q_\sigma = (2y_0 + 1)(2y_0 - 2y_1 - 2y_2 - 1),
$$
so both $p_\sigma$ and $q_\sigma$ take only odd values. Since $g_\sigma = 0$, the eigenvalue condition
\eqref{eqn:space_case2} reduces to $p_\sigma, q_\sigma \notin \{0, 1, -1\}$, that is,
$|p_\sigma|, |q_\sigma| \ge 3$.

For the self-covering degrees no such restriction exists: taking $y_0 = 0$, $y_1 = -\frac{n+1}{2}$,
$y_2 = \frac{n-1}{2}$ gives $q_\sigma = 1$ and $p_\sigma q_\sigma = n$, so every odd integer occurs, and
$$
SCD_2 = \{1, 3, 5, \dots\}.
$$
For the self-replicating degrees, write an odd composite number as $d = ab$ with $a, b \ge 3$ odd, and take
$y_0 = 0$, $y_1 = -\frac{a+1}{2}$, $y_2 = \frac{a-b}{2}$; then $p_\sigma = a$ and $q_\sigma = b$, so $d$ is
attained. Hence
$$
SRD_2 = \{9, 15, 21, 25, 27, \dots\}
$$
is the set of odd composite numbers.

Observe that the substitution $y_0 \mapsto -y_1$, $y_1 \mapsto -y_2 - 1$, $y_2 \mapsto y_0$ is a bijection of
$\mathbb{Z}^3$ that transforms $f_\sigma$ into $p_\sigma \cdot q_\sigma$; in particular, the two determinant
polynomials have the same set of values, and $SCD_1 = SCD_2$. Nevertheless, the two families contribute
differently to $SRD$: $SRD_2 \subsetneq SRD_1$, since, for example, $5 \in SRD_1$ but $5 \notin SRD_2$.
This shows that the eigenvalue condition has to be verified for each family separately and cannot be read off
from the determinant polynomial alone.

Note that these two families do not exhaust the sets $SCD(G_{24})$ and $SRD(G_{24})$. To compute them
completely, one must repeat the procedure for every row of
Tables~\ref{tab:case1_srdegrees0}--\ref{tab:case1_srdegrees308} and~\ref{tab:case2_srdegrees0} corresponding
to the group No.~24.
