The space group $I\, 2_1\, 2_1\, 2_1$ (ITA No. 24) is generated by:
$$
G_{24} = \left\langle\left(\begin{array}{rrr|r}
-1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & -1 & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \\
1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
\end{array}\right) , \left(\begin{array}{rrr|r}
-1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
1 & 1 & 0 & \frac{1}{2} \\
0 & 0 & -1 & \frac{1}{2} \\
\end{array}\right) , e_1 , e_2 , e_3\right\rangle
$$
In this case, the matrices $\mathcal{X}$ satisfying (PC) have a wide range of possible forms:
$$\left(\begin{array}{rrr}
x_{0} & 2 \, x_{0} & 0 \\
x_{1} & -x_{0} & x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1} \\
x_{2} & -x_{0} & 0
\end{array}\right) , \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
-2 \, x_{0} + x_{1} & 0 & 0 \\
x_{2} & -2 \, x_{0} + x_{1} + 2 \, x_{2} & 0 \\
x_{0} & 0 & x_{1}
\end{array}\right) , \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
x_{0} & 0 & 0 \\
-\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} + \frac{1}{2} \, x_{1} & 0 & x_{1} \\
-\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} + \frac{1}{2} \, x_{2} & x_{2} & 0
\end{array}\right)$$
$$\left(\begin{array}{rrr}
-x_{0} & -2 \, x_{0} & 0 \\
x_{1} & x_{0} & 0 \\
\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} + \frac{1}{2} \, x_{2} & x_{0} & x_{2}
\end{array}\right) , \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
x_{0} & 0 & 2 \, x_{0} \\
x_{1} & x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1} & -x_{0} \\
x_{2} & 0 & -x_{0}
\end{array}\right) , \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
x_{0} - 2 \, x_{1} & 0 & 2 \, x_{0} - 4 \, x_{1} \\
x_{2} & 0 & -x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1} \\
x_{1} & x_{0} & -x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1}
\end{array}\right)$$
Note that the $G_{24}$ is not symmorphic and is not present in the Appendix \ref{app:all_norm_dim3}. Instead, one
should seek the symmorphic group with the same point group, that is, $G_{23}$.

For the sake of succinctness, we consider only two of them. Firstly, take a closer look on the matrix
$A_1 = \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
x_{0} & 2 \, x_{0} & 0 \\
x_{1} & -x_{0} & x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1} \\
x_{2} & -x_{0} & 0
\end{array}\right)$, that can be found as the matrix No. 22 from the Table \ref{tab:norm_dim3_0}.
We omit the full system of congruences (\ref{eqn:cocycle_condition}) due to its complexity. Instead, here is the
smaller system (\ref{eqn:small_cong_system}) over integral variables $x_i$:
$$\left(\begin{array}{r}
x_{0} + 2 \, x_{2} \\
-\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} + x_{1} - x_{2} \\
-\frac{3}{2} \, x_{0} - x_{1} - x_{2} \\
2 \, x_{0} + 2 \, x_{2} \\
-x_{0}
\end{array}\right) \equiv \left(\begin{array}{r}
0 \\
\frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{1}{2} \\
-2 \\
1
\end{array}\right) \quad \mod 1$$
The solution is the set of matrices, satisfying the (PC)-(CC) conditions:
$$
A_1'=\left(\begin{array}{rrr}
-2 \, y_{0} - 2 \, y_{1} - 2 \, y_{2} - 1 & -4 \, y_{0} - 4 \, y_{1} - 4 \, y_{2} - 2 & 0 \\
y_{0} + y_{1} & 2 \, y_{0} + 2 \, y_{1} + 2 \, y_{2} + 1 & -2 \, y_{2} - 1 \\
2 \, y_{0} + y_{1} + y_{2} & 2 \, y_{0} + 2 \, y_{1} + 2 \, y_{2} + 1 & 0
\end{array}\right)
$$
The $A_1'$ falls into the case 2, since $\tau(A_1') = \tr(A_1') = 0$, and SRD has the form (\ref{eqn:space_case2}).
The determinant $\det(A_1')$ is a qubic polynomial of three variables No. 7 from the Table \ref{tab:case2_srdegrees0}:
$$
\det(A_1') = 4 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{2}^{2} + 4 \, y_{0}^{2} + 2 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{1} + 4 \, {\left(2 \, y_{0}^{2} + {\left(2 \, y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{1} + y_{0} - 1\right)} y_{2} - 1.
$$
We can factorize it and inspect the values it can attain:
$$
\det(A_1') = (2y_0 - 1)(2y_2 + 1)(2y_0 + 2y_1 + 2y_2 + 1).
$$
Now it is clear that $\det(A_1')$ can be any odd integer $n$ taking $y_0 = \frac{n+1}{2}, \, y_1 = -y_0, \, y_2 = 0$.
As a result:
$$
SCD = SRD = \{ 2k + 1 \, : \, k \in \mathbb{N}\}.
$$


Now consider the matrix $A_2 = \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} & 0 & x_{0} \\
x_{1} & \frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} + 2 \, x_{1} & -\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0} \\
x_{2} & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \, x_{0}
\end{array}\right) $. It contains rational coefficients, so we have to rewrite it to satisfy (LC):
$$A'_2 = \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
z_{0} & 0 & 2 \, z_{0} \\
z_{1} & z_{0} + 2 \, z_{1} & -z_{0} \\
z_{2} & 0 & -z_{0}
\end{array}\right).$$
This matrix is also present in the Table \ref{tab:case2_srdegrees0} as No.23.
Now the congruence system (\ref{eqn:small_cong_system}) over integral variables $z_i$ is similar to the previous one:
$$\left(\begin{array}{r}
0 \\
-\frac{1}{2} \, z_{0} + z_{1} - z_{2} \\
-\frac{3}{2} \, z_{0} - z_{1} - z_{2} \\
-2 \, z_{0} - 2 \, z_{2} \\
-z_{0}
\end{array}\right) \equiv \left(\begin{array}{r}
0 \\
\frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{1}{2} \\
2 \\
1 
\end{array}\right) \quad \mod 1,$$
which yields the matrix satisfying all the PC-CC conditions:
$$A_2'' = \left(\begin{array}{rrr}
-2 \, y_{0} - 1 & 0 & -4 \, y_{0} - 2 \\
y_{0} - y_{1} & -2 \, y_{1} - 1 & 2 \, y_{0} + 1 \\
2 \, y_{0} - y_{1} - y_{2} & 0 & 2 \, y_{0} + 1
\end{array}\right).$$

In contrast to $A_1'$, $A_2''$ falls into the case 1 and is block-triagonal up to the renaming of the basis vectors.
Although the minor $C'$ for $A_2'$ has only two variables and appears in the plane group No. 9
(check Table \ref{tab:norm_dim2_10}), the respective minor $C''$ for $A_2''$ suddenly has three variables. The $SRD$
has the form (\ref{eqn:space_case1}) for the following polynomials:
$$
p = -2 y_1 - 1, \quad q = 4 y_0^2 - 2(2 y_0 + 1) y_1 - 2 (2 y_0 + 1) y_2 - 1, \quad g = 0.
$$
This triple can be found in the Table \ref{tab:case1_srdegrees0}.

Since $g = 0$, the condition on eigenvalues by the Proposition \ref{propos:planar-srdegrees} reduces to $q \neq -1$. We
can factorize $q$ in the similar way to inspect what values $(p*q)$ can attain:
$$
q = (2y_0 + 1)(2y_0 - 2y_1 - 2y_2 - 1)
$$
Likewise $A_1'$, the $\det(A_2'') = p*q$ attains every odd integer $n > 1$ via
$y_1 = -\frac{n+1}{2}, y_2 = \frac{n-1}{2}, y_0 = 0$. It does not violate the condition on eigenvalues, since 
$q = 1 \neq -1$.
Moreover, we can now observe that the substitution $y_0 \mapsto -y_1, \, y_1 \mapsto -y_2 - 1, \, y_2 \mapsto y_0$ into the
polynomial $\det(A_1')$ yields exactly the $\det(A_2'') = p*q$. As a result:
$$
SCD = SRD = \{ 2k + 1 \, : \, k \in \mathbb{N}\}.
$$

Note that these two matrices do not represent the entire sets $SCD$ and $SRD$. To compute them completely, one must
repeat the provided procedure for every matrix from the $\mathcal{X}$.
